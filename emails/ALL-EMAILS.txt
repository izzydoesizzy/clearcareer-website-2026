Happy Saturday friends!

Confession. On Tuesday I went into the grocery store for three things. Milk, eggs, the specific bread Reyhan will actually eat. I walked out 50 minutes later with a plant, two kinds of hummus, and no bread. No eggs either.

That is EXACTLY what most job searches look like. No list. A vague sense of "I need a job." So you zig-zag every job board, pick up roles that look sort of useful, apply to some, abandon others, get tired, and close the laptop. Then you doom scroll LinkedIn and feel vaguely guilty about it.

The problem isn't effort. 50 minutes in a grocery store is a LOT of effort. The problem is that effort with no list is just wandering. And wandering is exhausting in a way that walking to three specific shelves never is.

**Here's the tactic.**

Make the list. Not a list of jobs. A list of companies. Here's how, in about an hour:

1. Open a blank sheet. Write down 5 companies you'd be genuinely happy to work for. Don't check if they're hiring. Doesn't matter yet.
2. Go to LinkedIn, search "people who work at [company]," and for each one find one person who does the job you want or manages that team. Name, title, link. Five rows.
3. Add 20 more companies by looking at who those five compete with, partner with, and get compared to. "Companies like X" into Google works great.
4. For each company, one line: why you. Not why them. Why YOU would be good there. If you can't write the line, cross the company off.
5. Sort by "want" not by "hiring." Then start at the top.

That's your list. Twenty-five shelves instead of the whole store. Every hour you spend on your search now goes to one of those 25 rows, and you can see progress because the rows fill in.
