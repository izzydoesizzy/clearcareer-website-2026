The whole conversation

Three parts. No surprises.

  1. About 15 minutes: the diagnosis. I ask questions and figure out where your search is breaking: targeting, outreach, interviews, or the offer itself. Usually it's one of the four, and usually you already half-know which.
  2. Then: the plan. I tell you what I'd build with you to fix it. Specific assets, in a specific order.
  3. And then, yes, the offer. If it's a fit, I'll tell you which program, what it costs ($497 to $4,997), and ask if you want to do it.

You can say no. People do, and we stay friends.

Book this if

  • You're targeting $100K+
  • You've been searching 3+ months, or you have an offer in hand
  • You're open to investing in your search in the next 30 days, if the plan makes sense
  • You're willing to do a 4-minute prep form before we talk

Don't book this if

  • You want free coaching. The session is free. The coaching isn't.
  • You're not in a position to invest right now.
  • You just want to pick my brain.

That's okay. Grab the free tools and the Saturday email, and come back when the timing's right.

Questions

Five things people ask before they book

Is it really free?

Yes. No card, no catch. 25 minutes of my time, and I ask for 4 minutes of yours on the prep form.

Is it a sales call?

Yes. And I say so up front because pretending otherwise wastes both our time. You get a real diagnosis for about 15 minutes. Then you get a real offer with real prices. Then you decide. That's the honest version, and it's the only version I run.

Can I bring my partner?

Yes. Please do, if they're part of the decision. It's a better conversation when the person you'd talk to afterwards is already in the room.

What should I bring?

A link to your resume. Your target salary. And your four numbers: applications sent, conversations had, interviews booked, offers received. Rough counts are fine. Those four numbers tell me most of what I need to know.

What if I need to cancel?

Use the cancel link in your confirmation email and rebook when it works. No hard feelings.
